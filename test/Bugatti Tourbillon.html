Bugatti Tourbillon

The Bugatti Tourbillon is a high-performance hypercar that represents the pinnacle of automotive engineering and design.
Known for its exceptional speed, advanced aerodynamics, and luxurious craftsmanship,
the Tourbillon is a symbol of exclusivity and innovation in the automotive world.
It features a powerful engine, cutting-edge technology, and a sleek, aerodynamic body that enhances its performance on both the road and the track.
The Bugatti Tourbillon is celebrated for its precision engineering, making it a thrilling choice for those seeking an unparalleled driving experience.

The Bugatti Tourbillon combines extreme performance with luxurious design. It is equipped with a powerful engine that delivers exceptional speed and acceleration, making it one of the fastest cars in the world.
The car's aerodynamic design and advanced engineering contribute to its high-speed capabilities, while its luxurious interior and attention to detail provide a comfortable and stylish driving experience.
The Bugatti Tourbillon is a limited-production vehicle, making it a rare and exclusive choice for automotive enthusiasts who appreciate both performance and luxury.
With its combination of speed, design, and craftsmanship, the Bugatti Tourbillon stands as a testament to the brand's commitment to pushing the boundaries of automotive excellence.

The signature Bugatti 'horseshoe' grille and corresponding air intake are even part of the crash structure; remove the front bumper and a carbon-fibre frame in the outline of the grille is visible.

Suspension is a multi-link design front and rear, claimed to be 45 per cent lighter than the Chiron's double-wishbone suspension, thanks to forged and 3D-printed aluminium components, and an "AI-developed 3D-printed hollow airfoil arm".

Carbon-ceramic brakes – with brake-by-wire technology – sit behind 20-inch front and 21-inch rear wheels wrapped in 285/35 front and 345/30 rear Michelin Pilot Sport Cup 2 tyres developed for the Tourbillon.

Rather than using wheels with thin spokes, from 1924 onwards Bugatti used wheels made of cast aluminium featuring eight flat and wide spokes, a removable wheel rim and an integrated brake drum for the legendary Type 35 racing car.

The simple design almost resembles a sculpture – a prime example of Ettore Bugatti’s aesthetic aspirations. However, the result was disappointing when the new wheels were first used at the Grand Prix in Lyon on 3 August 1924: several racing cars failed to finish as a result of technical issues.

However, the reasons were not the wheels, but the supplied tyres. Incorrect vulcanisation caused the tread to separate from several tyres.

Powering the Tourbillon is a new 8.3-litre naturally-aspirated (non-turbo) V16 petrol engine engineered in partnership with Cosworth, developing 735kW (1000 metric horsepower) and 900Nm, and revving to 9000rpm.

It is aided by three 250kW electric motors – two on the front axle, and one on the rear axle – for a system output of 1324kW (1800PS [metric horsepower]), up from 1177kW of the most powerful Chiron.

The new V16 engine has no turbochargers yet is almost as powerful as the Veyron's original 736kW/1250Nm version of the 8.0-litre quad-turbo W16 – and is about 150kg lighter.
